Disclaimer. This card is effective-date literacy — not legal advice, not a merchant POS scrape, not a “cash is legal tender everywhere” meme, and not a private-lawsuit funnel. We never invent a named store’s cash policy or allege a violation. Distinct from MD Grocery Price Ban (HB 895 personalized pricing ≠ HB 191 cash acceptance) and from Parking App Fee. Parking isn’t an essential good, so it’s out of scope for that reason. Public framing: Maryland HB 191 (Chapter 714 of 2026) — between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m., for in-person retail of essential goods totaling less than $300, merchants may not prohibit cash, require card-only, or surcharge cash for essential consumer goods (food incl. pet food; water/beverages; hygiene & cleaning; medicine/medical/PPE; fuel). A merchant may collect up to 4 cents extra when rounding a cash price under applicable law. Statutory exceptions: phone/mail/internet sales; vending machines/kiosks; merchants primarily selling food or beverages for on-premises consumption or a limited variety of gourmet/specialty foods; fuel sold where membership is required. Merchants may refuse denominations >$20 or offer a fee-free on-site cash→prepaid device (must accept cash if the device fails). Enforcement: AG Consumer Protection Division — after at least two opportunities to comply, up to $500 per violation, then up to $1,000 per subsequent violation — no private lawsuit CTA on this card. Effective Oct 1 2026. Sources: HB 191 enrolled PDF · The Bay Net Sep 5 2026 · Fiscal & policy note.
